# ACL story rewrite: contribution and scope contract

## Core contribution

A recurring machine--human lexical realization signature survives cross-generator reading, and its lexical fixed-token transport exceeds every one of 100 complete fitted-pipeline nulls in Llama 3.1 8B and Qwen3 4B; however, the tested pullback does not yield reliable component-selective control in free generation.

## Contribution hierarchy

1. Primary conceptual contribution: surface recurrence, hidden readability, fixed-token transport, and generated-language control are distinct empirical claims.
2. Primary empirical contribution: lexical transport exceeds 100 complete fitted-pipeline nulls in both target models; syntax is the internal counterexample because it is readable and directionally responsive without exceeding that null.
3. Supporting construct contribution: a declared, corpus-conditioned realization signature built from frozen linguistic measurements and tested across generators and elicitation interfaces.
4. Supporting method contribution: one construct is followed through held-out readouts, source-consistent Jacobian pullbacks, a complete fitted-pipeline null, and a separate generation audit.
5. Portable payoff: interpretability claims should be evaluated as an evidence chain rather than inferred from probe accuracy or a single intervention response.

## Claim ceiling

- Surface recurrence is supported within the four named source corpora, four named generators, and two elicitation interfaces. It is not a universal machine style or an authorship boundary.
- Cross-generator readouts predict independently measured continuous surface scores in two target-model families. Readability alone is not mechanism identification.
- Lexical fixed-token transport exceeds every one of 100 complete fitted-pipeline nulls in both models. This supports a transport trace on unchanged trajectories, not a universal causal mechanism.
- Syntactic transport is unresolved against the fitted-pipeline null despite strong directional responsiveness.
- Generated-language component selectivity is unresolved under the frozen single-layer, strength, prompt, decoding, and three-seed protocol. This is not evidence that all activation steering fails or that the exact behavioral effect is zero.

## Load-bearing cautions

- Proposition conditioning improves semantic matching but exposes only the machine continuation to the human-derived plan; prefix-only generation removes that interface while allowing more content divergence.
- Semantic cosine does not guarantee factual, pragmatic, or rhetorical equivalence.
- Discourse fails the same stability gate and remains an off-target audit.
- The fitted readout anchor is construction-internal and excluded from the confirmatory transport curve.
- Qwen syntax changes sign across replication seeds; Llama syntax has a positive target projection but unresolved selectivity; all four selectivity intervals include zero.
- The design covers English, named environments, five checkpoints, 32 Jacobian-fit pairs, one intervention layer and norm, one decoding regime, and three paired seeds.
- Automatic measurements inherit parser and inventory limits; reader perception is not measured; the signature is not suitable for authorship detection.
